Verilog & DSP-Based Control

Coursework · Digital design & ECEN 442/742 · Student designer · DSP lab coauthor

Project overview

I completed the class labs covering combinational logic, arithmetic, sequential circuits, timing, and state machines. In a separate DSP-based motion-control lab, my partner and I programmed a two-phase stepper motor and optical homing input using embedded C.

VerilogTestbenchesFSMsVivadoEmbedded CTI C2000

My contribution

I completed the Verilog design labs for class and worked with Lyric Haylow on the DSP-based stepper-motor lab, including the embedded C implementation, experiments, and report.

Technical implementation

Combinational and arithmetic RTL

Coursework included multiplexers, decoders and encoders, full adders, add/subtract logic, ALUs, and hierarchical carry-lookahead adders. The emphasis was building larger functions from smaller modules and checking outputs through simulation.

Sequential logic and control

The labs progressed to latches, flip-flops, synchronous arithmetic, counters, clock dividers, debounce circuits, and a digital clock. A traffic-light FSM connected state transitions and timing conditions with a synchronized external input.

Verification as part of the design

Testbenches and waveforms checked the logic behavior in simulation. The coursework also covered Vivado implementation artifacts and constraints connecting the design to FPGA hardware.

Stepper-motor excitation

The DSP lab configures GPIO pins and writes set/clear registers to produce four full-step excitation patterns. Half stepping inserts intermediate winding states for an eight-pattern sequence. Reversing the sequence reverses travel direction.

Homing through an external interrupt

An optical input on GPIO33 is connected to XINT1, with falling-edge detection and input qualification. The program installs the ISR through the interrupt-vector setup, enables the PIE/CPU path, stops the motor on the event, and acknowledges the PIE group. A shared flag coordinates the main loop with the ISR.

Electrical sequencing has a mechanical limit

The lab varied delays to observe where the scanner head stopped moving correctly. The report records jamming around 1,750 µs in full-step mode and 900 µs in half-step mode. These are observed failure thresholds in that setup, rather than a rated operating speed.

Development & improvements

Synchronize an external signal

An asynchronous input needs a deliberate boundary before synchronous state logic uses it. That is a hardware synchronization problem, separate from a CPU interrupt handler.

Coordinate firmware and mechanics

A correct GPIO pattern is only one requirement. The winding current and mechanical load also need enough time to respond before the next commanded state.

Full stepping → half stepping

Adding intermediate winding states changed a four-pattern full-step sequence into eight half-step patterns for finer positional increments. Both modes were tested against the scanner’s mechanical response as delays were reduced.

Final result & next steps

Coursework spanning RTL construction and simulation, plus a documented embedded C lab demonstrating full/half stepping, direction reversal, optical homing, and timing-limit experiments on a TI C2000-family platform.
