FreeRTOS & IMU integration

Ground-vehicle localization · MCU to Linux over UART

My contribution

My partner and I integrated an IMU through a microcontroller running FreeRTOS, then forwarded measurements over UART to a Linux-based robot computer for an EKF workflow alongside LiDAR.

MCU acquisition and the UART handoff

The embedded side handled sensor acquisition and preparation of measurements. FreeRTOS provided task scheduling and coordination for that work. The UART link separated the sensor-side firmware from the Linux estimation host.

The interface needed a consistent interpretation of measurement units and axes, a way to associate samples with measurement time, and enough throughput to keep up with acquisition. Synchronizing IMU and LiDAR time bases and calibrating the sensor-to-vehicle frames were central integration concerns.

Linux and estimation

The robot computer received the IMU stream alongside LiDAR processing. IMU information supported motion prediction, while LiDAR observations constrained the pose estimate in the EKF workflow.

The MCU split provided a dedicated acquisition boundary and added a transport and clock-alignment responsibility between the embedded firmware and Linux.

Result and documentation scope

This section records the IMU-to-MCU-to-Linux integration contribution. UART and FreeRTOS are confirmed in my project account. Exact MCU/IMU models, the EKF package, transfer rates, and measured localization performance remain unspecified until checked against the original hardware or firmware.
