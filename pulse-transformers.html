Iron Nitride Pulse Transformers

Hi-Re Technologies / Sandia National Laboratories · Simulation, design, testing & paper writing

Project overview

Conventional pulse transformers reserve space for a magnetic core and a separate encapsulant. This study explored a composite that performs both jobs, with the long-term goal of using the available device volume more effectively.

MagneticsSimulationPrototype designElectrical testingMaterials

My contribution

I carried out the simulations, design work, prototype work, electrical testing, analysis, and paper writing described in the manuscript. The paper is coauthored with Ryan Anderson, Todd Monson, and Melinda Hoyt.

Technical implementation

Design around the material

Iron-nitride/epoxy composites offer a different balance of permeability, saturation behavior, and manufacturability than a conventional core. The study adjusted winding density, wire size, and bobbin geometry around that balance while considering leakage inductance, resistance, coupling, and parasitic capacitance.

Compare material loading and processing

The study tested 25%, 35%, 45%, and 55% iron-nitride loading, with and without a particle-free epoxy precoat on the windings. Pourability and internal voids created a tradeoff: greater material loading did not automatically improve the electrical result.

Build and characterize prototypes

The prototypes used molded composite encapsulation and an elevated winding structure. Characterization included primary/secondary inductance and resistance, leakage measurements, temperature conditioning, and pulsed function testing. CT images and visual inspection helped interpret internal fill quality.

Measure the actual pulse

Function tests used a 20 V DC input with a pulsed MOSFET drive. The report includes tests with a 100 kΩ voltage load and a 0.1 Ω current load. In the reported voltage results, the non-precoated 35% sample produced the strongest peak: 218 V with a 1.986 µs pulse width.

Development & improvements

A more concentrated composite is not always better

Higher filler loading increased viscosity and complicated filling around the windings. The 35% sample without precoat outperformed higher-loading samples in the reported voltage tests.

Test the process assumption

A winding precoat was intended to improve infiltration and insulation. It did not improve the best electrical outcome in this study, making direct comparison more useful than assuming the extra process step would help.

Separate the target from the result

The design target was a 12.5× voltage step-up. The best reported peak corresponds to about 10.9× at 20 V input. The paper establishes a functional prototype; it does not report a demonstrated volume reduction.

Prototype results

The non-precoated 35% sample produced the highest reported output: 218 V from a 20 V input, with a 1.986 µs pulse width. The original 12.5× step-up target was not reached; the measured peak corresponds to about 10.9×.

Final result & next steps

Demonstrated functional voltage step-up with the integrated core/encapsulant concept and characterized the tradeoffs between filler loading, processing, and pulse performance. The initial step-up target was not reached, and further work was needed on fill quality and optimization.
